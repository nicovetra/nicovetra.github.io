Dose of Life

Essay · July 10, 2026

Getting a dose of all that life has to offer can be incredibly refreshing. Like the artist obsessed with a particular motif, one can forget the beauty that exists elsewhere. Dreaming of a single thing, tormented by an incomparable beauty. In the artist’s mind they have to capture it before it slips from their grasp, sinking in turbid water, unlikely to be found again.

Perhaps in some ways love is the chase of an inexplicable fixation. An insatiable appetite for a particular composition. And perhaps the feeling of not fully apprehending the beauty before us, as the artist might, is what keeps us returning to the same composition over and over.

Quite possibly this craze only takes ahold of us as reality slips from our fingers. A grazing chance encounter, a visit with someone we have no plans to see again, un la revedere, fara revedere1. This nebulous idea of what reality could be sinks into the abyss of our mind, rooting itself in a fanatical imagination. Like an artistic composition of the exact same object, over and over … unrelenting, the mind keeps designing new realities. Like Monet and the lily ponds, or Degas and the ballerinas2. All refusing to prune the branches of the motifs that light a spark. But like the bonsai tree that is not trimmed, beauty may become a shrub.

Perhaps those are the forces we have to follow if we are to pursue beauty like the great painters have: without worry of outside perception, but with a burning passion for the beauty we think we see in this world. I have not lived long enough to know true love. I have known great love, but I am not convinced one can know true love. I feel that love is a motif that no matter how many times we conceive, no matter how many times we paint the canvas, applying brushstrokes over and over, it still leaves us feeling as if we have not fully captured that which we are chasing.

I could be wrong. Maybe true love is like the painting an artist gets immediately. So beautiful they don’t dare lay another brushstroke. But if love felt that way I think we, like the artists, would bore and start anew in search of new motifs, ones which challenge us. For there is no shortage of beautiful motifs in the world around us. All one needs is a willingness to leave the confines of their own mind, and go into the world with an attentive eye. For beauty will shout and jump at the perceptive mind.

And if we tire of a motif so quickly then I do not think that could be true love. I think the truest of love … of obsession … of passion … of desire … is for the motif which constantly pushes us. The motif which engrosses our minds. Allowing us to reimagine ourselves in relation to this beautifully misunderstood object. Which no matter how hard we try to throw to the dark abyss of our minds keeps sparking a new fascination. Lighting up new exciting parts of ourselves we never would have explored alone.

Even when a motif seems lost, sunk to the clouded ocean floor, despair seldom helps. For a low tide can pull back the curtain on that which was lost. However, one must be willing to wade through nerve numbing, turbid water in search of that which matters. Even going as far as diving over and over in those wintry waters with no promise of anything but the muddy ocean floor. Only with a willing heart can we explore the motifs we may have once thought lost to the currents of the world.

We, like the artists, must be aware of the motifs that drive us mad and seek them out. As Kundera suggests “happiness is [our] longing for repetition”3. A realization of our leitmotiv. One that pushes us to change our style and approaches, applying different brushstrokes to depict the same reality. It is this mania of pursuit, through the same motifs, that opens the door for us to create beauty in this world, for ourselves, and those around us.
